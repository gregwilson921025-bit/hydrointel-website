What Tailwater does

One program for the daily work, the compliance work and the maintenance work. Every number is saved with the name of the person who entered it.

Daily operations

What operators do every shift.

Bench sheets

Enter a day's lab and field data on bench sheets laid out like your permit. Values save with your name, and corrections keep their history.

Rounds

Build your operator rounds once. Walk them in stages, and the readings go straight into the database. Print a blank sheet when you need paper.

Operator notes

Daily observations and standing items in one searchable log, so the next shift knows what you saw, what you did and what you're waiting on.

Current conditions and dashboard

Latest readings, trend tiles and what needs attention, with the time each reading came in.

Imports

Bring in Operator10 exports and spreadsheets. You review and map each column before anything becomes a reading.

Graphs and Graph Studio

Trend any reading over any period, side by side, and save the charts you use.

Process control

Tools that tell you how the plant is really doing.

Mass balance

Pounds in against pounds out. The gap tells you how good your sampling and flow metering really are, even when nothing is wrong.

Chemicals

What you feed, what it costs and what is left in the tank, with cost per million gallons treated so months and plants compare fairly.

Lab reagents

The EPA reagent record: vendor, catalog, batch and dates. Each test you record counts one off the bottle that expires first, so you reorder before the bench runs dry.

Compliance and reporting

The paperwork, ready before it is due.

Permit limits and violations

Each permit limit is checked against your data every month. Missing samples and exceedances are flagged before you file.

Sampling calendar

Reads every limit that has a monitoring frequency, works out how many samples the month owes, and puts them on your lab's pickup days.

Monthly reports and eDMR

The monthly filing comes first. Tailwater builds the export for your state's eDMR and the other reports you owe an agency.

Biosolids

Batches, fields and loads out, checked against the 40 CFR 503 ceilings, and the annual biosolids report built from what you logged.

Pretreatment

Your industrial users, their permits and limits. Significant non-compliance is worked out from the last six months of results, and permits expiring inside 90 days are flagged.

Surcharges

Set your sewer-use ordinance rates and Tailwater figures what each industry owes for strength over the domestic allowance.

Maintenance (CMMS)

Built to meet the EPA asset management and CMMS requirements for your system.

Work orders

Corrective and scheduled work orders, with priority set from asset criticality and total cost captured when the job is closed.

PM schedules

Recurring maintenance turns into work orders on its own when it comes due, so nothing waits on someone remembering.

Assets and equipment

Your equipment list by location, with criticality, history and the tasks that go with each piece.

Inventory and purchasing

Parts, storerooms, vendors and purchase orders. Every movement is logged, and parts below minimum are flagged.

Records you can trust

Built for the day an inspector asks.

Audit trail

Every entry, change and deletion is recorded with the person signed in. The record itself can't be edited.

Permissions

You decide who can enter data, correct it, delete it and change formats, by role or by person.

We set it up for you

We pull your permit from the EPA and build your bench sheets, permit limits, sampling calendar, plant diagram and equipment list. You review it with us before you go live.

Your best operator's knowledge stays at the plant

Every plant runs on things that aren't in the O&M manual: which pump to favor in wet weather, what the DO probe does when it's going bad, when to waste harder in the spring. That knowledge lives in a few people's heads, and it leaves when they retire or move on. Tailwater gives it a place to stay.

SME rules, in your own words

Your senior operator writes the rule like they'd tell a new hire. Their words are kept exactly. Tailwater makes a plainer version to help find the rule, and the author checks that it still means the same thing.

Who wrote it and where it applies

Every rule is stored with its author. A rule can apply to one plant or all of your plants, and old rules are retired, not lost.

What affects what

Cause and effect, written as sentences: when this reading goes up, that one moves, and here is why. The what-if screen on the plant drawing uses these rules to show a trainee what a change does downstream.

Plant Library

Drawings and schematics, SOPs and photos, each with a caption, kept with the plant so they're there when someone needs them.

Ask your data

Ask a question in plain English. Answers cite the reading and the time it was taken, and your plant's SME rules come first.

A certified operator decides

Tailwater recommends; a certified operator reviews before acting. Saving a rule records reviewed human guidance. It doesn't train a model on your data.
